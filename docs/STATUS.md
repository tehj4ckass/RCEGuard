## M2: Service and rule engine (R1–R6, R9), SQLite log

rule engine done and tested; service runs in a console. Acceptance test against Sysmon 15.22 passed on 2026-11-12: all simulator scenarios detected within 0.6–1.1 s of the log entry, the critical ones terminated. Installed as a Windows service (LocalSystem) the same test passed too, including the registration checks, the restart by Windows after the service process was ended, and terminating the program an exited child left behind. The build that saves its position in the Sysmon log passed the same test as installed service on 2026-11-12 21:59: all seven scenarios (0.1–1.1 s), among them `repeat` (three connections: one report, two counted), and all service checks, including the restart by Windows (after 62 s, from the third failure of a day on it takes that long) and the program started while the service was down (found 61 s later, right after the restart, child and client terminated). Two earlier runs of that day failed and were corrected: a first build received no event written after its start (wrong kind of subscription), and the test script counted old findings the service read at its start; see [KONZEPT.md](KONZEPT.md) section 14. After three reboots (2026-11-12 and 13, two of them for Windows updates, one cold start) the service started by itself 10–12 s after the kernel, continued in the Sysmon log where it had stopped, and then detected and terminated a simulator scenario within 0.8 s. Installing and removing again was checked on 2026-11-13 by comparing the machine before and after: after `uninstall.ps1` nothing of the installation was left (service, Program Files folder, data folder, Sysmon with its driver, event publisher and event log file, the note that the Sysinternals licence was accepted, the registrations of the service as a source of the Windows Application log). What stays are the entries the service wrote to the Application log. A service that cannot start is removed again: `scripts\install-test.ps1` showed that on 2026-11-13 for a service build with a missing file and for a configuration that is not valid JSON (service, folder and event sources gone, sensor still there, nothing restarted by Windows), and a normal installation worked right after.

**The same run ended with a half-removed Sysmon:** the Sysmon service crashed while `sysmon -u` was uninstalling it. A second `sysmon -u` could not finish the job either: the service that was left is protected by Windows and can only be removed by itself. `uninstall.ps1` now checks on the machine whether Sysmon is gone, tries once more, and for this dead end has the switch `-RemoveStuckSysmonService`. That way out worked on the test machine on 2026-11-14 (switch, restart of Windows, `uninstall.ps1` again): nothing of RCEGuard and Sysmon was left. Right after that `install-test.ps1` passed completely (2026-11-14 00:24, 21 of 21 checks): both failed starts, the installation without a fault, and the removal, this time without a crash of Sysmon.

**Not yet verified:** the service after the machine wakes from hibernation.

**Known gap in the sensor:** Sysmon often does not record a connection of a client that ends within a few seconds, see [DETECTION.md](DETECTION.md#connections-r2-are-slower-and-can-be-missed). Toast moved to M4

## M3: Prevention: firewall profiles, Exploit Protection, kill switch

built and accepted with the simulator; **not tried with a real client**. Built: the service writes one block rule per profile to Windows Firewall (`"firewall": true`), locks a profile down after a finding that terminates its client (`"lockdown": true`) and ends a lockdown with `RCEGuard.Service.exe --release`; `uninstall.ps1` removes the rules. Both switches are off by default. `scripts\prevention-test.ps1` passed on the installed service on 2026-11-15 (20 of 20 checks): Windows Firewall took the rule, the allowed address connected and the other one did not, the lockdown blocked everything and was still there after a restart of the service, `--release` ended it, and no rule was left at the end.

**Seen in that run:** a connection the firewall blocks leaves no finding, see [PREVENTION.md](PREVENTION.md). Not verified: `uninstall.ps1` with rules in place; the real client. Exploit Protection: `install.ps1 -BlockChildProcesses` turns on "Do not allow child processes" for the client, `-AllowChildProcesses` takes it back. Checked with the simulator on 2026-11-15 15:56 as part of `prevention-test.ps1` (27 of 27 checks): Windows refused when the simulator started a program, and after taking the setting back no entry was left and the guard terminated the started program again. A first run that day failed at taking it back (the documented `Set-ProcessMitigation -Remove -Disable` reported no error and changed nothing) and led to the scripts reading every change back and removing their own entry from the registry. `uninstall.ps1` takes the setting back too: `install-test.ps1` passed completely on 2026-11-15 15:58 (23 of 23 checks) with an installation that had it on; afterwards nothing of the installation was on the machine. Not verified: the real client with the setting. Plan and decisions: [KONZEPT.md](KONZEPT.md) section 15

## M4: Tray UI, toast, ntfy, heartbeat

started. Built: the service sends findings that alert or terminate to an ntfy topic, and reports about itself (cannot run, was ended unexpectedly, stopped while the client runs); `RCEGuard.Service.exe --notify-test` sends a test message. Tested with a stand-in for the network, and the published service sent a real test message to a listener on the test machine.

**Not yet run:** the installed service with a real ntfy server. Also built: the tray application (`RCEGuard.Tray`): an icon in the notification area that shows whether the guard runs, a window with the findings, a pop-up for new findings, and "Release" for a lockdown, which asks Windows for an administrator. It only reads what the service stores. Tested without its user interface; the window and the icons were drawn into pictures and looked at, and the application was started once for a few seconds. `install.ps1` installs it to `C:\Program Files\RCEGuard Tray`, lets Windows start it for every user at sign-in and starts it; `uninstall.ps1` removes it. `install-test.ps1` confirmed that on 2026-11-15 17:12 (25 of 25 checks): installed, registered, running without administrator rights although it was started from an administrator console, and gone after the uninstall. Next to the installed service it showed the findings of `prevention-test.ps1` as Windows notifications (2026-11-15 17:17, seen by the owner). In that run the Snipping Tool could not be used while the notifications came; why is not known (the Windows logs show nothing that RCEGuard blocked), and since then the pop-ups are bundled. The release from the window was checked with `scripts\tray-test.ps1` on 2026-11-15 17:38 (9 of 9 checks; the owner clicked "Release ..." and confirmed the administrator question, the script checked): the lockdown ended, the release command had been started by the tray application, no firewall rule was left, the running service noticed 6 s later, and the test client started again.

**Not yet done:** the start at sign-in; the real client. Plan and decisions: [KONZEPT.md](KONZEPT.md) section 16

## Client file: check and optional patch

built on 2026-11-16 by the owner's decision (the concept had kept patching outside the project). RCEGuard says when the client file has a section that is both writable and executable (at the end of `install.ps1` and on request with `--check-client`; a standing warning in `guard.log` and in the tray window was taken out again the same day), and `scripts\patch-client.ps1` takes the execute right away from the client's `.zdata` section or gives it back. Tested on made-up files and on a copy of a real client in a temp folder, in PowerShell 7 and 5.1 (26 checks: the patched copy differs in exactly one byte, undo gives back the identical file, six kinds of unexpected files are left alone).

**Tried once with a real client (2026-11-16):** the patched copy starts and shows the login screen, and crashes right after the password is entered, twice in two tries, with an access violation at the first address of `.zdata`. The crash report shows code that was loaded at run time (it belongs to no file) calling into that section: on that server, logging in involves running code there, so **the patched client cannot be used on that server**. Whether the patch stops an exploit is still not known. See [CLIENT-PATCH.md](CLIENT-PATCH.md)

## M5: Learning mode, R7, R8

started. Built: learning mode for a profile (`"learning": true`): for that client nothing is terminated, locked down or blocked by the firewall rule of the profile, a connection to an address that is not on the allowlist is noted instead of alerted, and the service collects where the client connects to, which host names it asks for, where it creates files outside the game folder and which other programs it opens. `RCEGuard.Service.exe --learned` shows the notes and suggests what to add to the allowlist; nothing is taken over automatically. Tested with unit tests and by replaying the events of the M2 acceptance run through the built service. Also built: R7 judges what the access rights allow. Opening another program with rights to change it (write its memory, start threads in it) is an alert, reading its memory a log entry, anything less an info entry; programs on `allowedProcessAccess` may be looked at without an entry, and the learning mode suggests that list too. The simulator has `--open-process` for it; the real Sysmon recorded its three variants with exactly the rights asked for (2026-11-15, seen through the installed service of the previous build).

**Not yet run:** the installed service in learning mode and with the new R7; a name service event of the real Sysmon (the notes read it from the documented format); the real client, so nobody knows yet which programs it opens and with which rights. Also built: R8. The service measures the processor time of every running client (from the process list of Windows, without opening the client) and alerts when a client keeps more than `cpuAlertCores` cores (default 2.0) busy on average over `cpuAlertSeconds` (default 300); whether the client is in the foreground is not looked at. Tested with made-up processor times in the guard loop, and the reading of the process list against this machine.

**Not yet run:** R8 on the installed service with the simulator's `--cpu`; how many cores the real client keeps busy is not known, the default is an assumption. `scripts\learning-test.ps1` checks all of this on the installed service. First run on 2026-11-15 20:08: 19 of 23 checks passed. Passed on the installed service: nothing terminated or locked down while learning, R1, R3, R7 (read: log, rights to change: alert) and R8 (13.1 cores over 10 s) reported, the Explorer suggested for `allowedProcessAccess`, and after learning an unknown address alerted and a started program was terminated again.

**Failed, all four for one reason outside the guard:** Sysmon recorded neither of the two connections of the learning part (4 s and 12 s after it had loaded its configuration; each client stayed alive for 8 s), so there was nothing to note or suggest; later in the same run it recorded all three. Why is not known. The name query of that run was recorded, and replaying the run's events shows that the notes read it correctly and suggest the name. The test now asks Sysmon after every connection whether it recorded it, tries up to three times and lists what was missed. Second run on 2026-11-15 20:24: 23 of 24 checks passed. Learning worked from end to end on the installed service: both connections noted, the host name suggested as a name and the address as an address, and after the suggestion was taken over the learned address connected without a finding.

**The one failed check is the sensor again:** Sysmon recorded only 4 of the 9 connections of that run, and none of the three tries for the host name after learning. The misses do not follow the loading of its configuration (the two connections right after it were recorded this time).

**So the known gap is larger than measured before: about half of the connections of a client that lives 8 seconds were missing.** A measurement of its own followed (`scripts\sensor-probe.ps1`, 2026-11-15 20:38): of ten connections closed at once Sysmon recorded nine, of ten connections kept open for 8 s it recorded eight.

**Keeping the connection open does not close the gap**; the guard reported every connection Sysmon recorded (17 of 17). The cause is not known.

**Built against it:** the service also reads the connection table of Windows for every running client and takes from it what Sysmon did not record, once a connection has been open for 5 seconds; such a connection is judged and learned like a recorded one. Measured on the installed service on 2026-11-15 21:53 (`scripts\sensor-probe.ps1`): of ten connections kept open Sysmon recorded seven, and the guard saw all ten, three of them through the table, 7 to 9 seconds after the client had started; nothing was reported twice. Of ten connections closed at once Sysmon recorded nine and the guard saw those nine: a connection that is closed again within those seconds still depends on Sysmon alone. The tray application shows learning mode: a blue shield with a ring, a headline that says the client is watched, not protected, and per learning profile a button that shows what was learned, with the suggestion. Tested without the user interface, and the window and the icon were drawn into pictures and looked at; **not yet seen next to the installed service.** Taking the suggestion over stays an edit of `appsettings.json` as administrator.

**Still open in M5:** a full session with the real client. `install.ps1 -Learning` sets it up; it ran as administrator on 2026-11-15 22:26 and the real client was then started once, for about two and a half minutes (start, login, realm): no finding alerted. Right after its start the client opened 36 other running programs with the right to read their memory (R7, logged; what the concept expected of an anti-cheat scan), it made four connections, two of which Sysmon did not record and the guard took from the connection table, and three of the four addresses had been asked for by name. A configuration taken from that start (three host names, one address, learning off, `killEnabled: false`) then ran for two real play sessions on 2026-11-16, about 25 minutes together: **no finding alerted**, no connection went to an address outside the allowlist, R8 did not report, and the client had used 0.98 processor cores on average over the 13 minutes of the second session (read from the process list of Windows), against the 2.0 cores R8 reports above. Still open: the two hours of normal play the concept asks for, and turning `killEnabled` on. Plan and decisions: [KONZEPT.md](KONZEPT.md) section 17
